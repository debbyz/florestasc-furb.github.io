Aim:

To promote science communication and environmental education, especially for schools

FlorestaSC's forest education activities promote science communication, aimed especially at schools, based on the scientific knowledge produced by FlorestaSC studies.

The team develops educational materials and training pathways with teachers and students to spark curiosity, raise socio-environmental awareness and encourage sustainable action in and for the forests of Santa Catarina, through schools and the university.

One of the proposals is the Forest Library, a set of booklets on the forests of Santa Catarina co-written with teachers from the Santa Catarina state school system.

Forest Library

The Santa Catarina Forest Library collection is educational material co-written with teachers from the Santa Catarina state school system, covering topics on the forests of Santa Catarina linked to the state's Base Curriculum.

The material consists of five (5) booklets that support training pathways with teachers and students to spark curiosity, raise socio-environmental awareness and encourage sustainable action in and for the forests of Santa Catarina, through schools and the university.

Each booklet covers one topic on the forests of Santa Catarina. See the five topics in the image:

ACCESS AND DOWNLOAD THE SANTA CATARINA FOREST LIBRARY COLLECTION

In April 2023 our group began meeting to plan and collaboratively write the booklets of the Santa Catarina Forest Library. The meetings are monthly, held both in person and online. Besides FlorestaSC teachers and researchers, schoolteachers from Blumenau and the surrounding region take part.

Virtual reality apps

Virtual reality apps were developed to share knowledge about the forests of Santa Catarina, especially in schools.

From 2024 to 2026, FlorestaSC and FURB's Technology Development and Transfer Laboratory (LDDT) ran the subproject 'FlorestaSC: science communication on forests using digital technologies based on virtual, augmented and alternate reality'. It was coordinated by Prof. Dr. Maurício Capobianco Lopes and funded by FAPESC.

To share knowledge about the forests of Santa Catarina interactively in schools, three (3) virtual reality apps were developed:

  • FlorestaSC RA: an augmented reality app for interacting with explanatory leaflets about the forest [Learn more; Download]
  • FlorestaSC RVi: an immersive reality app set in the Floresta Rene Frey park (Fraiburgo/SC) [Learn more; Download]
  • FlorestaSC na Trilha: an alternate reality app for visits to the São Francisco de Assis Park (Blumenau/SC), offering deeper knowledge about the park. [Learn more; Download]

FlorestaSC RA: augmented reality app

FlorestaSC RA is a question-and-answer app that uses augmented reality. It makes learning about the forests and species of Santa Catarina more dynamic and fun.

With FlorestaSC RA you can interact with five (5) tree species from our forests and learn more about them: imbuia, xaxim-bugio, araucaria, palmiteiro and embaúba.

Download FlorestaSC RA on Google Play:

https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=br.furb.ldtt.FlorestaSCRA

Print the species photos (markers), or point your phone camera at them, to use with the app.

Download the markers

FlorestaSC RVi: immersive reality app

FlorestaSC RVi offers a unique virtual reality (VR) experience in the René Frey Forest. The app opens the doors to one of Fraiburgo's most important ecological sanctuaries, combining tourism, history and scientific rigour.

Leave the outside world behind and step into one of the rare native forests within an urban area in Brazil. Here, conservation is not just a concept — it is a living experience.

Download FlorestaSC RVi on Google Play:

https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=br.furb.ldtt.FlorestaSCRVi

FlorestaSC na Trilha: alternate reality app

FlorestaSC na Trilha is an educational app that brings together nature and technology to strengthen forest education and science communication in non-formal settings. Developed for the São Francisco de Assis Municipal Nature Park in Blumenau (SC), it uses alternate reality to guide the experience on the trails, promoting learning in three dimensions: knowing in, about and for the forest.

Knowing in the forest values immersion, careful observation and investigation in direct contact with the environment.

Travelling exhibition

Our Forests: travelling exhibition

We are running an exhibition that travels to schools, universities, shopping centres and events across the state, giving an overview of FlorestaSC – the Forest and Floristic Inventory of Santa Catarina – and its achievements.

Any owner or manager of a space with public circulation can host the 'Our Forests' exhibition, always free of charge!

Information and bookings by e-mail at florestasc@furb.br or by phone at +55 (47) 3221-6100

Over all these years of research, FlorestaSC has addressed the scientific community, publishing books, papers and more. Now we are also turning to the general public – people interested in what has been discovered so far about the forests of Santa Catarina. With this in mind, we designed an exhibition presenting our findings.

The idea is to present our findings in a simpler way and inform the public about nature-related topics and terms.

About the exhibition:

It has 3 display tables with field equipment, published books, seeds, wood and plants, plus 13 information panels and others with photos from the FlorestaSC collection;

Space required: at least 60 m², covered and sheltered from strong wind.

Topics covered:

How a forest inventory is done

Even before FlorestaSC/IFFSC, two great researchers studied the forests and plants of Santa Catarina between 1950 and 1970. FlorestaSC/IFFSC began in 2007 with flora surveys. Here we show how it was done.

How much forest is left, and other findings

A forest inventory includes quantitative and qualitative surveys. Here we show how much forest is left in the state and its condition (main species and main concerns).

Forest types in Santa Catarina

Santa Catarina lies within the Atlantic Forest biome, which includes different forest types (vegetation physiognomies). They differ because of variations in climate, soils, relief and geographic location, explained here.

Plant types

Tropical and subtropical forests like ours have the greatest variety of plants. In five years of FlorestaSC surveys a high species richness was found. Here we present the features of each plant group and the number of species found in each.

Epiphytes

Epiphytes are typical plants of tropical and subtropical forests that use trees for support (without harming them). Many people are passionate about this group, with its own beauty and a very different way of surviving.

Ferns and lycophytes

Ferns and their allies – horsetails, tree ferns, maidenhair ferns – are well known, since many are used to decorate porches and gardens. Many do not look like the typical house fern, and it is in the forest that we find a great variety of forms. We show a little more about them here.

The herbarium: a large plant collection

The herbarium is a botanist's paradise: it is where specimens of plants collected in the field are kept. Herbaria have their own rules; we show how the FURB Herbarium works, where all plants collected by FlorestaSC are stored.

Forest succession

Did you know that degraded forests can recover? But it is not a quick process – it can take centuries. That is why conserving forests matters. Here we show how forest succession works.

The partnership between plants and animals

The partnership between plants and animals is often so specific that the loss of one partner can lead to the extinction of the other. Plants provide shelter and food for animals, and animals take part in pollination, seed dispersal and plant protection – a partnership shaped by many millions of years of evolution.

Genetic diversity

Genetics is present in every living being and underlies the evolution of every species over time. Plants are no different: loss of genetic diversity harms the future of species. Find out what FlorestaSC found about the genetics of threatened species in Santa Catarina.

Using forest resources

A team from EPAGRI, the Santa Catarina Agricultural Research and Rural Extension Company, in partnership with FlorestaSC/IFFSC, visited farmers' homes across the state to find out which species were most used for timber, medicine, food and ornamental purposes. Here we show the species most often mentioned by farmers – perhaps with potential for future use.

Impacts on the forest

It is no surprise that many activities affect the forests of Santa Catarina – but do you know which are the most common? FlorestaSC/IFFSC surveyed them, and they are summarised here.

What can we do?

Although we have lost a significant part of our forests, we still have great natural wealth – we just need to know how to look after it. Here are suggestions for action by the State and by each of us. Reflect on it.

BIBLIOTECA DA FLORESTA CATARINENSE

The Santa Catarina Forest Library is an educational resource made up of five booklets on different themes about the forests of Santa Catarina. It aims to support learning pathways with teachers and students to spark curiosity, raise socio-environmental awareness and encourage sustainable action on and in our forests, through schools and universities.

➤ Register to download the Santa Catarina Forest Library collection.

The Santa Catarina Forest Library collection includes the following booklets:

  • Volume 1: Forest cover
  • Volume 2: Forest riches
  • Volume 3: Uses of forest products and services
  • Volume 4: Human impacts on the forest
  • Volume 5: Forest stocks
